# Buy Tradelines: What to Check Before You Buy (2026)

**Short version:** An authorized user tradeline adds an existing credit card account's history to your credit file. It can help some people with thin or young files. It will not erase late payments, collections, or charge-offs, and no honest seller can promise a score change or a loan approval. Below is what to check before you spend money, and how to tell whether a tradeline is likely to help you at all.

## First: will a tradeline help your situation?

Answer this before you look at a single listing.

**It may be worth considering if:**
- Your file is thin or young (few accounts, or short history).
- You have no recent late payments, collections, or charge-offs.
- You have a mortgage, auto loan, or refinance coming up and want to add older, positively reporting history.

**It is unlikely to help if:**
- You have recent late payments, collections, or charge-offs. Those items can outweigh an added account.
- Your utilization is high on your own cards. Paying those balances down usually does more.
- Your file already has accounts older and with higher limits than the line you're looking at.

If you're not sure which group you're in, that's a good reason to talk to a person before buying. At EzeCreditServices, that's Matt, Kathleen, or the rest of the team in our Raleigh office. If a tradeline isn't likely to help, we'll tell you.

## How a tradeline actually works

Understanding the mechanism protects you from most bad purchases.

1. A cardholder with an older account in good standing adds you as an authorized user.
2. The card issuer reports the account to the credit bureaus on its own schedule, usually after the monthly statement closes.
3. The bureaus update your file. The account may then appear on your credit report.
4. The standard term is two reporting cycles, about 60 days.

Two things follow from this. First, **timing is set by the card's statement and reporting dates, not by the day you pay.** Second, what the account does to your score depends on the scoring model, the lender, and the rest of your file. Nobody can know that in advance.

## 7 things to check before you buy

### 1. Bank
The issuing bank determines how the account is reported and how much it matters to the lenders reading your file. Inventory should name the bank before you pay.

### 2. Credit limit
Higher limits generally mean lower reported utilization on the account. Compare against the limits already on your file. A line that is smaller than what you already have adds little.

### 3. Account age
Older, seasoned accounts add more history. A 12-year-old Chase line with a $20,000 limit tells you something. "A powerful, aged tradeline" tells you nothing. Insist on the actual number.

### 4. Reporting date
This is the date the issuer reports to the bureaus. It tells you when the account could first appear. If a seller won't show it before payment, treat that as a warning.

### 5. Order-by date
Each line has a last day to order so the purchase can be completed before the reporting date. Missing a card's cycle can delay reporting by about a month. If you have a closing date, count backward from it and leave room for bureau updates, which is a reason to start early. It is not a reason to rush.

### 6. The written guarantee, and what it covers
Read it. A posting guarantee is a promise that the tradeline will appear on your report, subject to its terms. For example, our guarantee requires the purchase at least five days before the reporting date. It is not a guarantee of a score change or a loan approval, and anyone offering one is offering something no one controls. Confirm the current terms in writing before you buy.

### 7. Who answers the phone
Can you reach a real person with a name, before and after you pay? Is there a physical office? When something goes wrong, that matters more than anything on the website.

## Price: what's normal?

Prices vary with the bank, limit, and age of the account. Typical lines run around $450, with a range of roughly $200 to $2,000. Very cheap lines are often cheap for a reason, such as a low limit, a young account, or unclear posting terms. Check current inventory for actual pricing.

If you need more time on a line, extensions are available one reporting cycle at a time for half the original price, subject to current terms.

## Red flags

- **Hidden inventory.** If you have to "call for details" about the bank, limit, or reporting date, ask why.
- **Score promises.** "Add 100 points" and "jump to 700+" are not claims anyone can back up.
- **Countdown timers and "only two left."** Real urgency comes from a real order-by date, not a clock on a web page.
- **CPNs or a "new credit identity."** Credit privacy numbers sold as a fresh start are a fraud risk. The FTC has warned consumers about them. Stay away.
- **No written guarantee.** If the promise isn't in writing, you don't have one.
- **"We'll fix your credit."** Tradelines add positive history. They do not remove negative items.

## What happens after the term ends

When you're removed as an authorized user, the account may be deleted from your credit file. What that does to your scores depends on the bureau, the scoring model, and what else is in your file. Don't assume a benefit stays in place after removal, and don't buy from anyone who says it will.

## Questions people ask

**How long until a tradeline posts?**
It depends on the card's statement and reporting dates. After the issuer reports, the bureaus update your file. Your order-by date tells you the latest day to buy for a given cycle.

**Is buying a tradeline legal?**
Becoming an authorized user on someone's account is a common, legal credit arrangement. Practices differ widely, though. Primary tradelines, CPNs, and anything that misrepresents who you are or what you owe are different matters entirely. If you're applying for a loan, be honest on the application. When in doubt, ask the lender or an attorney.

**How many points will my score go up?**
We don't guess. Scoring models and lenders vary, and your own file decides how much any new account matters.

**Do I need more than one?**
Not necessarily. Compare the line against the accounts already on your file. If it isn't older and higher-limit than what you have, more lines won't change that.
