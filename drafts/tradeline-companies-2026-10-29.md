# Tradeline Companies: What to Compare and What to Ask Before You Choose

**Short version:** Tradeline companies vary widely. The best way to compare them is to look at what each one shows you before you pay, what it promises in writing, and whether you can reach a real person. Use the table below as a starting point.

We sell tradelines, so we have a stake in this topic. We've written this to be useful whichever company you pick, and we haven't named or ranked any competitors.

## Compare these 8 things

| What to compare | Look for | Be cautious if |
|---|---|---|
| **Inventory detail** | Bank, limit, account age, reporting date, and order-by date visible before payment | You must call or email to learn basic details |
| **Written guarantee** | A written posting guarantee, with its terms and conditions in plain language | The guarantee is verbal, vague, or promises score changes |
| **Timing explanation** | Clear explanation of statement date, reporting date, and bureau update | "Fast" or "quick" with no specific dates |
| **Score claims** | No score promises; honest about uncertainty | "Boost your score 100 points" or "700+ guaranteed" |
| **Who it's for** | Says plainly who may not benefit | Presents the product as right for everyone |
| **People** | Named staff, a phone number, a physical office | Only a web form or chat widget |
| **Pricing** | Clear prices tied to bank, limit, and age; clear extension pricing | Very cheap lines with no details; unexplained fees |
| **Identity practices** | Authorized user tradelines only | Any mention of CPNs, "new credit identity," or primary tradelines you can "buy" |

## Questions to ask before you pay

Copy these into an email or ask them on the phone. A good company will answer them directly.

1. **What exactly is the guarantee, and can I see it in writing?** Ask what it covers and what conditions apply, such as how far before the reporting date you must buy.
2. **What are the bank, limit, account age, reporting date, and order-by date on this line?**
3. **Which bureaus does this account report to?**
4. **What happens if the line doesn't post?** Is the remedy a refund, a replacement, or something else?
5. **What happens when the term ends?** Ask what the removal does to the account on your file. If the answer is "the benefit stays," ask for specifics.
6. **Can I talk to someone before I buy, and who?**
7. **If this line isn't likely to help my file, will you tell me?**
8. **What's the extension policy and price?**

Question 7 is the most revealing. A company that has never turned down a sale is telling you something.

## How to read reviews

- **Look for named staff.** Reviews that mention a specific person, such as "Kathleen answered my questions" or "Matt walked me through it," are harder to fake than generic five-star praise.
- **Read the middle ratings.** Three- and four-star reviews usually explain the real trade-offs.
- **Check for posting complaints.** Posting problems are the most common reason people leave bad reviews. See how the company responded.
- **Treat Reddit threads as a mix.** Useful for patterns; not reliable on any single claim. Verify what you read.

## Red flags that should end the conversation

- Promises about score gains or loan approval
- Countdown timers or "only 2 left" pressure
- CPN or "new credit identity" language
- No written guarantee
- Refusal to show the bank, limit, or reporting date before payment
- No way to reach a person

## Do you need a tradeline at all?

Often the best advice a tradeline company can give is "not yet." Tradelines add history to a file. They don't remove late payments, collections, or charge-offs. If your main problem is recent negative items or high utilization, work on those first. See [Authorized User Tradelines: How It Works and Who It May Not Help](/authorized-user-tradelines).
